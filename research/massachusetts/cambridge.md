## What prices a permit in Cambridge

Three pages of one Inspectional Services section — Building / Electric / Plumbing Permits —
each printing a two-column table of items and fees, each headed by the same three ordinance
warnings. There is no PDF: the pages *are* the schedule, and every figure below is a table
cell read from them.

**The three ordinance headers, repeated verbatim on all three pages:**

- **Section: 15.04.010 Established — Work Started Prior to Obtaining Permit:** "Where work
  for which a permit is required by this code is started or proceeded with, prior to
  obtaining such permit, **the fee specified shall be tripled**, but payment of such triple
  fee shall not relieve any person from fully complying with the requirements of this code…"
- **City Ordinance 15.08. & 15.12 Penalty for Starting Work Prior to Obtaining Permit:** the
  same sentence for work under the Electric Code / Plumbing Code.
- **City Ordinance 15.08.020 & 15.12.020 Co-Work Completed By Others:** a person or firm
  (other than electrical or plumbing contractors or public service agencies) who employs
  permanent electricians or plumbers "may obtain a permit covering the work done by such
  permanent electrician/plumber for a period of time to be determined in each case. The fee
  … will be charged in the same manner as if separate permits had been given for each job."

The plumbing page adds two lines of its own: "Performing work without a permit: Triple the
permit fee", "Failure to call for final inspection within 5 days of completion — $50.00",
"Re-Inspection Fee — $50.00".

## Readings this dataset depends on

**(a) The rate rounds up — the phrase is printed four times.** Every cost-priced building
row reads "$20.00 per $1,000.00 **or fraction thereof** of construction cost", and the
Moving buildings row prints "rounded up to the next thousand" in its own words. The engine
therefore sets `incrementCents: 100_000` on each of them: $18,750 is nineteen whole steps,
not eighteen and three quarters. This is the opposite of Boston's sheet, 3 miles east, which
prints no such phrase and prorates — the two readings are asserted side by side in the two
cities' tests, which is the point of putting them in one state. Sheet Metal's "$50 fee plus
$25 per each 100 linear feet" is the one row where neither phrase appears and a hundred feet
is a **rate unit** rather than a block — $25.00 a hundred feet is 25 cents a foot — so that
row prorates on the run, at $0.25 per linear foot.

**(b) The $50.00 minimum is a floor on the rule, not a floor on the permit.** "$50.00
minimum fee" is printed with each rate, so it is the rule's own `minimumCents`: a $2,000 job
is 2 × $20.00 = $40.00 charged at $50.00, and a $1,000 exempt job is 1 × $15.00 = $15.00
charged at $50.00 as well.

**(c) The exemption is a fact about the building, not about the job.** "Exemption: $15.00
per $1,000 … for three residential dwelling units or less" is selected by
`custom.residential_units` at 3 or below, with the standard $20.00 rule written as the
negation of that condition so a reader who does not state a unit count is charged the
standard rate rather than nothing. The word is *exemption* in the City's own column, but the
row still charges — at $15.00 rather than $20.00 — so it is modelled as a rate, not as a
waiver.

**(d) The four cost rows are one rule because they are one arithmetic.** "All new
construction, Repairs & Alterations", "Amendments to plans", "Demolition of buildings &
structures" and "Preliminary permits for foundation" print the identical pair of rates, so
charging them as four rules would be four ways of computing the same number; the single rule
names all four rows in its description. Moving buildings is *not* folded in: it prints
$15.00 — the exempt rate — for every job regardless of unit count, so it is its own rule
gated on `custom.moving_buildings`.

**(e) Plan review is not charged, and the cell that would say so is ambiguous.** The table
prints one cell — `$100 included in building permit fee$50.00` — with no separator and no
legend. The most likely reading is a $100 plan review folded into the permit fee (which
would cost nothing extra) with a $50 figure belonging to a row the export lost; the other is
that plan review is $100 *and* $50 for something else. Neither reading can be derived from
the page, so no plan-review rule exists and the cell is quoted verbatim on the page and in
this record. What the schedule does *not* contain is as important: no percentage of any
kind, no technology fee, no state surcharge.

**(f) Electrical is a price list, not a branch.** Unlike Boston's sheet, Cambridge's rows
carry no "when… / when…" language: they are line items of a list and they stack. A permit
for a new service and four receptacles pays the service row *and* the receptacle rows, which
the worked example on the electrical page shows. The receptacle rows are five prices for one
line of "Receptacles/air conditioners, check amps", so the rating is a fact
(`custom.receptacle_amps`) over one count of `custom.outlets`, with 15 amps — the first row —
as the default when no rating is stated.

**(g) "$50.00 for 5 fixtures, $5.00 for each additional fixture" is a block with a
five-fixture allowance.** One to five fixtures cost $50.00 together and each one after the
fifth costs $5.00 more — the `per_unit` shape of `baseCents: 5000`, `thresholdUnits: 5`,
`centsPerUnit: 500` — not five separate $10 fixtures. The fixture list the schedule prints to
define the count is quoted on the plumbing page.

**(h) The triple fee is a penalty on a fee, and is named rather than charged.** All three
pages carry the same sentence — the fee "shall be tripled" where work starts before the
permit — which multiplies a fee that has not been computed. It is stated in the requirements
and on every page, never modelled.

**(i) No state surcharge exists in Massachusetts.** No instrument read for this pass — the
City's three fee pages, the DPW announcement, or the State's code — adds a levy to a local
Cambridge permit, and the pages print none. `componentType` across the jurisdiction is
therefore `base`, except the two re-inspection rows, which are `inspection`.

## Effective dates on record

The three fee pages print **no date at all**. The only dated instrument read is DPW's
announcement "Changes to Construction Permit Fees Effective January 1, 2024" (published
2023-12, page last modified 2024-01-03), and it does **not** name ISD's rows: the items it
lists are Sidewalk Obstruction Permits (monthly fee), the Utility Inspectors' Overtime Fee
and a note that "certain construction permit fees have been revised", after "a review of
construction-related permit fees that have not been increased in a decade", with "a complete
revised fee schedule … available … on January 1, 2024". Because the announcement cannot be
tied to the building, electrical or plumbing rows this site charges, the schedules and their
rules carry **the read date (2026-09-25) as their effectiveFrom** — the same choice Buffalo's
undated sheets record — and the announcement is held as a source with its own date so the
possibility that these rows moved in 2024 stays visible.

## Requirements read (for the requirement rows)

From the three pages and their ordinance headers: work may not start before the permit
issues, and if it does the fee is tripled (§15.04.010 for building, Ordinances 15.08 and
15.12 for electrical and plumbing); an employer of permanent electricians or plumbers may
take a period permit for that work, "charged in the same manner as if separate permits had
been given for each job" (§§15.08.020, 15.12.020); a final inspection must be called within
five days of completion or $50.00 is charged, and a re-inspection is $50.00; "All service
equipment greater than 1,000 amps [is] to be tested by certified testing firm"; and the fee
table itself is preceded by the City's own warning that a tripled fee "shall not relieve any
person from fully complying with the requirements of this code". Department contact, printed
in the footer of every page: Inspectional Services Department, 831 Massachusetts Avenue,
Cambridge, MA 02139, 617-349-6100; counter hours Monday 7:00–8:30 a.m. and 3:30–5:30 p.m.,
Tuesday–Thursday 7:00–8:30 a.m. and 3:00–3:30 p.m., Friday 7:00–8:30 a.m. and 11:00 a.m. –
12:00 p.m.

## Not modelled (named on the page, never papered over)

- **The triple fee** — a multiplier on a fee, printed on all three pages and quoted in the
  requirements.
- **Certificates of Occupancy and Certificates of Inspections** — seven rows priced on
  units, rooms, occupants and square feet, read and transcribed here, not attached to a page.
- **Signs** ($10 per $100 of sign cost rounded up, $20 minimum): the rate is a percentage of
  a *sign cost*, which no basis in this engine reads — `valuation` is the project's, not the
  sign's — so the row is named rather than charged.
- **Building licenses** ($50 initial, $50 renewal, $60 late, $10 a year after), **annual
  sign inspections** ($10), **publicly subsidized dwelling inspections** ($100) — licensing
  and inspection tariffs rather than permit fees.
- **Sheet metal** is modelled (it is a permit row priced in linear feet) but **re-inspection
  of code violations at $25.00 and $100 & $5 a unit** are named, because which of the two
  re-inspection figures applies to a given visit is not stated on the page; the plumbing
  page's own $50.00 re-inspection is charged, as its own row, on that page.
- **Electrical rows priced by a count this site does not collect**: motors (by horsepower),
  heating units (by kilowatt), air conditioners (by ton), ductless units, solar arrays,
  maintenance permits, data/low-voltage/audio-visual work by occupancy, bounced cheques and
  shutdowns. Each is transcribed in the table above.
- **Plumbing rows for individual devices** — back water valve, hose bib, irrigation meter,
  cross-connection devices, dedicated systems, water piping per floor, sanitary waste and
  vent per floor, sprinkler heads and standpipe valves — read, priced on this page's own
  table, and named rather than charged: each would need a fact of its own, and the fixture
  block is the row a plumbing job actually starts from.
- **Plan review**, per reading (e): no charge, ambiguous cell, quoted verbatim.

## Open questions

1. **The effective date of these schedules.** The pages print none, and the DPW
   announcement that dates January 1, 2024 names sidewalk obstruction permits and utility
   inspector overtime rather than ISD's rows. If ISD's own revision notice is found, every
   figure here gains its real date.
2. **The plan-review cell.** `$100 included in building permit fee$50.00` — one cell, two
   amounts. A call to ISD (617-349-6100) would settle whether plan review is $100 inside the
   permit fee, $50, or both; the model charges nothing either way and says so.
3. **Whether the receptacle rows are per ampere rating or per device type.** The schedule
   prices 20-ampere receptacles as "disposal, dishwasher" and 50-ampere as "range, hot water
   heater/tub" — the rating *and* the appliance name the row. The model takes the rating,
   which is the word the line begins with ("check amps").
4. **Meters, generators and alarms are charged per item or once.** The page prints "Meter,
   each $5.00" (per item, charged per meter) but "Generator $100.00" and an alarm price with
   no "each" (charged once per permit); if ISD charges generators and alarms per device, both
   are undercharged on multi-device work.
